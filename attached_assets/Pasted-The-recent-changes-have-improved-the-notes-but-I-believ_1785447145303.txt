The recent changes have improved the notes, but I believe we've reached the point where the remaining issues are no longer prompt problems—they are extraction and provenance problems.

Please do **not** change the SOAP note layout, section order, formatting, headings, diagnosis numbering, Care Plan structure, Follow-Up structure, or overall writing style.

Instead, I want to improve how the structured extraction classifies provider actions before the writer ever generates the note.

The current extraction still promotes discussions into decisions and loses the provider's clinical reasoning.

Going forward, every provider statement should be classified into **exactly one** of these categories:

**1. Discussed**

* Education provided.
* Risks and benefits reviewed.
* Options reviewed.
* General counseling.
* Pathophysiology explained.
* Questions answered.
* No treatment decision was made.

Examples:

* Discussed omega-3 fish oils.
* Reviewed risks and benefits of statins.
* Explained the role of estrogen in cardiovascular health.
* Discussed testosterone injection versus topical therapy.

These items should never appear as active treatment plans.

**2. Recommended**

* The provider recommends a treatment.
* The patient has not yet agreed.
* Shared decision-making is still occurring.
* Multiple acceptable options remain.

Examples:

* Recommended considering a low-dose statin.
* Recommended Mediterranean diet.
* Recommended calcium score screening.

These should remain documented as recommendations, not completed plans.

**3. Decided / Initiated**

* A mutual treatment decision was made.
* Medication was prescribed.
* A diagnostic test was ordered.
* Follow-up was scheduled.
* This is the actual agreed-upon plan.

Only items in this category should populate:

* Assessment & Plan
* Care Plan
* Follow-Up
* Medication changes

Nothing from another category should automatically appear there.

**4. Future Consideration**

* Conditional plans.
* Contingency planning.
* Possible future medication changes.
* Escalation plans based on response.

Examples:

* Consider testosterone injections if topical absorption is inadequate.
* Consider metformin if insulin resistance persists.
* Increase progesterone if 100 mg is ineffective.
* Transition back to an estrogen patch if compounded therapy is unsuccessful.

These should remain future considerations and should never be rewritten as today's plan.

Additionally, I want the extraction to preserve provider reasoning.

For every treatment decision, capture:

* Why the provider made the recommendation.
* Which symptoms supported it.
* Which laboratory findings supported it.
* Which history supported it.
* Which alternatives were discussed.
* Why those alternatives were not selected.
* Any contingency plan if treatment fails.

The writer should generate the Assessment & Plan from this structured reasoning rather than trying to infer it from the transcript.

For example:

Incorrect:
"Plan: Start a statin."

Correct:
"Discussed elevated triglycerides, borderline ApoB, ASCVD risk of 2.7%, and cardiovascular risk reduction strategies. Recommended Mediterranean diet and omega-3 supplementation. Discussed that a low-dose statin would also be reasonable; patient may elect either approach through shared decision-making."

Another example:

Incorrect:
"Plan: Start metformin."

Correct:
"Discussed metformin as a future option for insulin resistance if lifestyle measures, GLP-1 therapy, and metabolic improvement are insufficient."

Another example:

Incorrect:
"Plan: Start testosterone injections."

Correct:
"Reviewed topical versus injectable testosterone. Patient elected to begin compounded topical estrogen/testosterone therapy. Injectable testosterone may be considered if absorption or symptom response is inadequate."

Finally, please stop generating ROS findings that were never documented.

The ROS should be extraction-only. If the patient did not explicitly report or deny a symptom, it should not appear in the ROS.

I believe the writer itself is now close. The next improvement should come from making the structured extraction preserve provider reasoning and decision provenance instead of flattening every discussion into a treatment plan.
