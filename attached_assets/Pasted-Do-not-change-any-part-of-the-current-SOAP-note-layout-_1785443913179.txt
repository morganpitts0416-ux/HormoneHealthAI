Do not change any part of the current SOAP note layout, section order, headings, formatting, diagnosis numbering, Care Plan format, or Follow-Up format. This request is only to improve the accuracy and completeness of the clinical content generated within the existing layout.

The current failure appears to be upstream in structured extraction and normalization, not primarily in the final note-writing prompt. The extraction process is flattening the encounter, losing chronology and clinical reasoning, and confusing current medications with treatment decisions.

Please update the extraction and normalization stages as follows:

1. Preserve chronology exactly.

The extraction must separately identify:

* symptoms and when each symptom began
* prior treatments and when they were stopped
* current treatments at the start of the encounter
* treatment changes decided during the encounter
* future or conditional treatment considerations
* follow-up timing

Do not combine separate timelines into one generalized statement.

For example, a hysterectomy 16 years ago, longstanding hot flashes, and weight gain beginning around age 50 must remain separate facts. Do not rewrite this as “all symptoms began after hysterectomy.”

2. Strictly separate medication status.

Every medication must be assigned one status:

* Current at encounter start
* Newly started today
* Dose increased today
* Dose decreased today
* Discontinued today
* Discussed only
* Future consideration
* Patient unsure of medication or dose

Never place a newly prescribed dose in the Current Medications section as though the patient was already taking it.

Example:

* Current tirzepatide: patient reports a low starter dose, exact dose uncertain
* Plan: increase tirzepatide to 5 mg weekly

Do not convert those into “Current medication: tirzepatide 5 mg.”

3. Do not normalize uncertain medications into a different drug.

Preserve the patient’s actual wording when medication identity is uncertain.

Example:

* If the patient says Nexium, document esomeprazole/Nexium.
* Do not change Nexium to pantoprazole.
* If the exact medication cannot be confirmed, mark it as patient-reported or uncertain.
* Never pair two different medications as though they are interchangeable, such as “pantoprazole (Nexium).”

4. Extract provider clinical reasoning as structured data.

For every treatment decision, extract:

* Decision made
* Supporting symptoms
* Supporting laboratory findings
* Relevant history
* Provider rationale
* Alternatives discussed
* Why an alternative was not selected
* Counseling provided
* Monitoring plan
* Future or conditional next step

Example:

Decision:
Start transdermal estradiol patch.

Supporting evidence:
Surgical menopause after hysterectomy, estradiol 5.8, persistent vasomotor symptoms, fatigue, low libido, agitation, and prolonged absence of hormone therapy.

Route rationale:
Transdermal estrogen was selected to avoid first-pass hepatic metabolism because of fatty liver findings, dyslipidemia, inflammation, and cardiovascular risk factors.

Alternatives discussed:
Estrogen injection and oral estrogen.

Alternative not preferred:
Oral estrogen was not preferred because of hepatic first-pass effects.

Future consideration:
Consider progesterone later for persistent sleep disturbance or agitation. Do not add it automatically because the patient does not have a uterus.

5. Preserve provider sequencing and conditional plans.

The note must distinguish between:

* treatment started now
* treatment intentionally deferred
* treatment that may be added only if certain symptoms persist

Example:

* Start estrogen now.
* Do not start progesterone simultaneously.
* Consider progesterone later if sleep disturbance or agitation remains after assessing estrogen response.
* Defer testosterone because hemoglobin and hematocrit are elevated in a current smoker.
* Reconsider testosterone after smoking cessation and improvement in erythrocytosis risk.

Do not collapse these into a vague statement such as “consider adding progesterone.”

6. ROS must be extraction-only.

Do not generate, infer, complete, or embellish ROS findings.

Only include a symptom or denial in ROS when the patient explicitly reported or denied it during the encounter.

Prohibited examples:

* “Denies depression” unless the patient explicitly denied depression.
* “Denies abdominal pain” unless the patient explicitly denied abdominal pain.
* Adding system findings based on diagnoses, medications, laboratory findings, family history, or provider counseling.

A symptom mentioned by the patient may be included, but it must not be transformed into a denial or broader review that was never performed.

7. Medical, surgical, social, and family history must remain factual.

Do not generate placeholder text such as “None of these.”

Do not infer that no medical history exists simply because a complete history was not discussed.

Use:

* “Not fully reviewed during this encounter,”
* omit unsupported items,
* or preserve only the history explicitly stated.

8. Do not treat laboratory interpretation as a diagnosis unless the provider did.

Preserve the provider’s actual interpretation.

Examples:

* Elevated ferritin with normal circulating iron was discussed as potentially related to inflammation and metabolic liver dysfunction, not automatically iron overload.
* A1C of 5.5 was interpreted by the provider as evidence of metabolic dysfunction/insulin resistance despite not meeting the laboratory threshold for prediabetes.
* Elevated hemoglobin and hematocrit were considered likely smoking-related.
* FIB-4 was low risk.
* Lp(a) was elevated and clinically important because of the family history.
* hs-CRP was markedly elevated and interpreted as systemic inflammation.

9. The assessment must use all relevant supporting evidence.

For each diagnosis, the extraction should provide the writer with:

* symptoms
* objective laboratory evidence
* relevant history
* risk factors
* prior treatment
* treatment rationale
* counseling
* monitoring plan

Do not reduce a diagnosis to one symptom and one lab when the transcript contains substantially more supporting evidence.

10. Preserve clinically meaningful discussions that affect treatment.

The extraction must not discard major topics simply because they were lengthy counseling discussions.

For this encounter, clinically meaningful topics included:

* surgical menopause and severe hypoestrogenism
* transdermal versus oral versus injectable estrogen
* why oral estrogen was not preferred
* progesterone not required for endometrial protection because she has no uterus
* possible later progesterone use for sleep or agitation
* testosterone deferred because of elevated hemoglobin/hematocrit and smoking
* tirzepatide starter-dose failure and dose escalation
* smoking cessation with bupropion
* elevated Lp(a)
* family history of premature cardiovascular disease
* ASCVD risk
* coronary calcium screening
* elevated hs-CRP
* fatty liver and low-risk FIB-4
* low folate
* vitamin D optimization
* omega-3 and CoQ10 recommendations
* staged supplement plan
* protein goal
* four-week medication follow-up
* eight-week laboratory follow-up

The final note does not have to reproduce every educational analogy or casual conversation, but it must preserve the clinical conclusion, rationale, decision, and follow-up associated with each meaningful topic.

11. Improve normalization without overwriting source truth.

The structured extraction must remain the source of truth.

Normalization may classify and organize extracted information, but it must not:

* invent facts
* replace uncertain facts with confident ones
* change medications
* merge timelines
* convert recommendations into current medications
* convert discussed options into completed decisions
* generate diagnoses unsupported by the encounter

When the transcript, extraction, and normalized data conflict, the system should flag the conflict for QA rather than allowing the writer to guess.

12. Strengthen QA verification.

The QA stage should compare the finished note directly against the transcript and structured extraction and reject or revise the note when it finds:

* a medication listed as current that was started or increased during the visit
* an incorrect medication name
* a fabricated ROS denial
* a fabricated medical-history statement
* merged or altered timelines
* missing major treatment decisions
* a future consideration presented as a current plan
* a deferred treatment presented as started
* an unsupported diagnosis
* incorrect units, such as vitamin D written as 5,000 mg instead of 5,000 IU
* missing route, dose, frequency, or follow-up when explicitly stated
* duplication between the Assessment and Care Plan that introduces contradictory instructions

Please make these changes only to the extraction, normalization, provenance handling, and QA logic needed to improve content accuracy.
