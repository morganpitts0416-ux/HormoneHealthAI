We need to stop making incremental changes to the current SOAP note generation prompt and instead redesign the documentation architecture.

After reviewing our current pipeline, I do NOT believe the evidence extraction or normalization pipeline is the problem. In fact, I think those layers are very strong. The issue is how that information is ultimately transformed into a SOAP note.

The current prompt has grown into hundreds of individual rules, validations, and exceptions. At this point, many of those rules are competing with one another and the AI is beginning to summarize encounters instead of documenting them. I do not want another revision of the existing prompt. I want to redesign the philosophy of the note generator while preserving all of the existing evidence extraction, medication normalization, diagnosis inference, historical context, medication state gates, bundle matching, and QA safety features.

Core Philosophy

ClinIQ should not be generating "summaries."

ClinIQ should generate a complete medico-legal medical record.

The transcript is the source of truth. The AI's responsibility is to faithfully reconstruct the encounter into professional medical documentation while preserving the provider's clinical reasoning and every medically relevant discussion.

The note should answer this question:

"If another provider reads this chart five years from now, would they understand exactly what happened during this encounter, why each decision was made, what was discussed, what was deferred, and what the provider's clinical thought process was?"

That is the documentation standard I want ClinIQ to become known for.

Documentation Philosophy (These should become the permanent ClinIQ documentation standards)

These principles should NEVER be overridden by Teach June.

1. Never summarize an encounter.

Convert the entire encounter into professional medical documentation.

Every medically relevant discussion should be documented somewhere in the chart.

3. Document the provider's clinical reasoning.

The Assessment & Plan should not simply list diagnoses and treatments.

Another provider should be able to understand WHY every decision was made.

Each diagnosis should contain:

Diagnosis Name (with ICD code)

Clinical Rationale

The rationale should generally be 3–5 sentences explaining:

Why this diagnosis applies
Symptoms supporting it

Relevant previous evaluations

Clinical thought process
Differential diagnosis when appropriate
Why treatment was initiated, changed, continued, or stopped

This is where the provider's thinking is documented.

4. Future planning is part of today's documentation.

One of ClinIQ's distinguishing features should be preserving future clinical thinking.

Whenever future treatment options are discussed—even if they are NOT initiated—they should be documented under:

Future Considerations

Examples:

"If patient tolerates oral progesterone, testosterone replacement will be discussed at next follow-up."

"If vasomotor symptoms fail to improve, will consider increasing estradiol dose."

"If dizziness persists despite hormone optimization, neurology referral will be considered."

"If insulin resistance remains uncontrolled, metformin may be initiated."

These discussions are extremely valuable when reviewing previous notes and should never be lost.

This section should contain actionable bullet points that clearly outline:

Laboratory testing

Monitoring instructions

Expected side effects

Follow-up timing

Patient responsibilities

This should be detailed enough that, if requested, it could be printed and handed directly to the patient.

Provider Voice

The note should ALWAYS be written from the provider's perspective.

Never use phrases like:

"The provider stated..."

"The provider recommended..."

"The provider discussed..."

Instead use active documentation language:

Discussed...

Reviewed...

Recommended...

Initiated...

Discontinued...

Continued...

Explained...

The note should read exactly as though the provider personally documented the encounter.

Speaker Attribution

Correctly identifying who is speaking is absolutely critical.

Patient concerns should remain patient concerns.

Provider conclusions should remain provider conclusions.

For example:

Patient:

"I wonder if this could be hormone related."

Document:

Patient expressed concern that symptoms may be hormone related.

Provider:

"I actually don't think this is hormone related."

Assessment:

Clinical presentation appears unlikely to be hormone related based on current findings.

The AI must never confuse patient speculation with provider assessment.

Medication State Fidelity

Medication actions must be documented accurately.

Examples:

"Let's start estrogen."

→ Initiated estrogen therapy.

"Let's increase your tirzepatide."

→ Increased tirzepatide dosage.

"Let's stop spironolactone."

→ Recommended discontinuation of spironolactone.

"Let's continue current testosterone."

→ Continue testosterone therapy without change.

The AI must never confuse initiation with continuation or continuation with discontinuation.

Preserve Diagnostic Journey

For new patients especially, previous evaluations are often medically important.

If a patient states they previously:

Saw Internal Medicine
Saw Cardiology
Saw ENT
Saw Neurology
Had CT imaging
Had MRI
Had CAC scoring
Had carotid ultrasound
Had colonoscopy
Had endoscopy
Had previous laboratory work

Those evaluations should be incorporated into the HPI because they demonstrate what has already been investigated and what conditions have already been reasonably excluded.

This protects the provider by accurately documenting previous diagnostic workup and clinical reasoning.

The HPI should document those prior evaluations as part of the patient's medical journey, not merely as isolated historical facts.

Teach June Architecture

I do NOT want Teach June changing ClinIQ's documentation philosophy.

Instead, I want three distinct layers:

Layer 1 — ClinIQ Core (Locked)

This defines our permanent documentation standards.

It should never be overridden.

Examples:

Preserve clinical reasoning
Preserve future considerations
Preserve medication changes
Preserve patient concerns
Preserve diagnostic journey
Preserve provider education
Preserve shared decision making
Preserve medically relevant conversations
Never summarize encounters

These are part of ClinIQ's identity.

Layer 2 — Clinic-Level Documentation Preferences

Entire organizations may customize things such as:

Preferred diagnosis bundles
Preferred physical exam defaults
Preferred ROS format
Preferred terminology
Preferred templates
Layer 3 — Teach June (Individual Provider Preferences)

Providers may customize style without reducing documentation quality.

Examples:

Keep HPI shorter
Use different formatting
Use my preferred diagnosis bundles
Prefer paragraph format
Prefer bullet format
Use my preferred hormone diagnosis terminology
Always include normal physical exam unless otherwise specified

However, Teach June should NEVER be allowed to remove medically relevant documentation or reduce the fidelity of the medical record.

Final Goal

I want ClinIQ to become known for producing the most complete, clinically useful, and medico-legally defensible AI-generated medical documentation available. Our competitive advantage should not be that we create shorter notes—it should be that we faithfully reconstruct the entire encounter into a medical record that preserves the provider's clinical reasoning, documents every medically relevant discussion, and serves as a reliable reference for future care. The evidence layer should continue to provide factual accuracy, while the generation layer should focus on transforming that evidence into documentation that reads as though it was written by an experienced clinician. The QA layer should verify completeness and fidelity rather than forcing increasingly complex prompt logic.
