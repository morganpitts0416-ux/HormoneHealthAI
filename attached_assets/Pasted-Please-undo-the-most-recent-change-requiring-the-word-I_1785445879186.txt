Please undo the most recent change requiring the word “I” as the explicit subject of every provider action.

The intended note voice is provider-authored clinical voice with an implied first-person subject. It should sound as though the treating provider wrote the note, but it should not repeatedly say “I.”

Correct style:

* Reviewed laboratory results with the patient.
* Discussed the risks, benefits, and alternatives of hormone therapy.
* Recommended starting transdermal estradiol.
* Counseled regarding smoking cessation.
* Will repeat labs in eight weeks.
* Consider progesterone later if sleep disturbance or agitation persists.
* Testosterone was deferred because of elevated hemoglobin and hematocrit in the setting of current tobacco use.

Incorrect third-person narrator style:

* The provider reviewed laboratory results.
* The clinician recommended estrogen.
* Morgan explained the treatment options.
* It was discussed that the patient should stop smoking.

Incorrect repetitive explicit first-person style:

* I reviewed her laboratory results.
* I discussed estrogen therapy.
* I recommended starting a patch.
* I counseled her regarding smoking cessation.
* I instructed her to return in eight weeks.

The governing rule should be:

“Write from the treating provider’s perspective using standard provider-authored clinical documentation. Use concise active clinical verbs with an implied first-person subject. Do not refer to the treating provider in the third person, and do not require or repeatedly insert the pronoun ‘I.’”

Please update all enforcement layers consistently:

1. Generation prompt
2. Voice and narrator rules
3. QA check 25
4. Fidelity audit
5. Any post-processing or rewrite logic

Remove any rule stating:

* “Write with ‘I’ as the subject for every provider action.”
* “Explicit first-person ‘I’ voice is mandatory.”
* Implied-subject constructions such as “Reviewed,” “Recommended,” “Discussed,” or “Counseled” are prohibited.

Those implied-subject constructions are the preferred clinical style and must be allowed.

The QA logic should flag:

* “The provider said…”
* “The provider explained…”
* “The clinician recommended…”
* “Morgan discussed…”
* “It was explained by the provider…”

The QA logic should not flag:

* “Reviewed…”
* “Discussed…”
* “Recommended…”
* “Counseled…”
* “Advised…”
* “Will monitor…”
* “Plan to repeat…”
* “Consider adding…”

When correcting third-person narration, rewrite it into concise implied-first-person clinical voice:

* “The provider explained the risks” → “Discussed the risks.”
* “The provider recommended an estradiol patch” → “Recommended an estradiol patch.”
* “The clinician advised smoking cessation” → “Counseled regarding smoking cessation.”
* “The provider stated that labs would be repeated” → “Will repeat labs in eight weeks.”

Do not automatically convert those sentences to:

* “I discussed…”
* “I recommended…”
* “I counseled…”
* “I will repeat…”

Occasional use of “I” may be preserved only when it is clinically meaningful or directly reflects a documented provider judgment, such as:

* “I suspect the elevated hemoglobin and hematocrit are related to smoking.”
* “I do not recommend oral estrogen given her hepatic and cardiovascular risk factors.”

Even in those cases, explicit “I” should be used sparingly and should never become the default sentence structure.

Do not change the SOAP note layout, headings, section order, formatting, diagnosis numbering, Care Plan structure, or Follow-Up structure. This change applies only to narrative voice.
