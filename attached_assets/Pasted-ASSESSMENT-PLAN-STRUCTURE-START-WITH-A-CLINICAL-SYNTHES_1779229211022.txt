ASSESSMENT & PLAN STRUCTURE

START WITH A CLINICAL SYNTHESIS PARAGRAPH
Every moderate/high-complexity encounter should begin with a concise but clinically sophisticated synthesis paragraph (typically 3–6 sentences).

This paragraph should:

summarize the overall clinical picture
connect symptom patterns, labs, medications, and longitudinal history
explain provider reasoning
reflect diagnostic nuance and uncertainty when appropriate
explain WHY treatment decisions were made
sound like an experienced clinician thinking through the case

The synthesis paragraph should preserve chronology and causality.

Example style:
“Symptoms remain consistent with progressive perimenopausal hormonal transition contributing to fatigue, sleep disruption, mood variability, decreased libido, and reduced exercise recovery. We discussed the relationship between fluctuating estrogen/progesterone levels and her symptom pattern, along with the role of testosterone optimization in energy, motivation, body composition, and sexual health. Given persistent symptoms despite lifestyle optimization efforts, shared decision-making led to initiation of low-dose testosterone therapy and nightly progesterone support while continuing metabolic and nutritional optimization.”

AFTER THE SYNTHESIS PARAGRAPH:
Use numbered assessment items organized by CLINICAL CONDITIONS OR DOMAINS.

Each numbered section should contain:

DIAGNOSIS / CONDITION TITLE

Then:

supporting symptoms
relevant labs/findings
clinical justification
provider interpretation
treatment rationale
differential considerations when appropriate
patient-specific education/counseling naturally integrated into the discussion
shared decision-making details when relevant

DO NOT isolate “Counseling/Education” into a separate subsection.
Instead, weave counseling naturally into the clinical reasoning.

Then end each numbered section with:

Plan:

exact medications and dosing
start/continue/hold/stop decisions
testing ordered
referrals
monitoring
follow-up timing
future considerations if applicable

EXAMPLE STRUCTURE:

Perimenopausal Hormonal Transition / HSDD

Patient reports progressive fatigue, sleep disruption, decreased libido, reduced exercise recovery, and mood variability consistent with early perimenopausal hormonal fluctuation. Lab findings and symptom progression support evolving hormonal transition despite preserved menstrual cycling. We discussed the role of testosterone in libido, motivation, muscle maintenance, and overall quality of life, along with the benefits of progesterone for sleep support and hormonal stabilization. Risks, expected response timeline, and dose-adjustment expectations were reviewed.

Plan:

Start testosterone cypionate 10 mg IM weekly
Start progesterone 100 mg nightly
Reassess symptom response in 2–4 weeks
Repeat hormone labs in approximately 6–8 weeks depending on clinical response
IMPORTANT ORGANIZATIONAL RULES
Keep related symptoms together under the most clinically appropriate condition/domain
Do NOT create separate diagnosis headings for every symptom or discussion point
Do NOT over-fragment related conditions into isolated buckets
Preserve all clinically relevant symptoms, diagnoses, medications, and decisions discussed during the encounter
Differentiate clearly between:
active decisions made today
future considerations
differential diagnoses
exploratory discussion
pending workup
CAUSALITY & LONGITUDINAL REASONING

Preserve temporal and clinical nuance.

If symptoms pre-dated a medication, do NOT inaccurately label the medication as the sole cause. Instead use nuanced phrasing such as:

“may be exacerbating”
“temporally worsened following”
“superimposed on chronic symptoms”
“possibly contributing to”

The note should reflect real clinical reasoning and longitudinal thinking rather than simplistic symptom-to-diagnosis mapping.

GOAL

The goal is:

clinically intelligent documentation
medicolegally complete documentation
longitudinal reasoning
realistic provider voice
strong treatment rationale
preservation of all clinically relevant details

WITHOUT:

excessive fragmentation
repetitive filler language
insurance-template formatting
isolated counseling subsections
over-diagnosing every conversational topic
