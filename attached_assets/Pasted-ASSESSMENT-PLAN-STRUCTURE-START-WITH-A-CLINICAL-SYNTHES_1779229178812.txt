ASSESSMENT & PLAN STRUCTURE

START WITH A CLINICAL SYNTHESIS PARAGRAPH
Every moderate/high-complexity encounter should begin with a concise but clinically sophisticated synthesis paragraph (typically 3–6 sentences).

This paragraph should:

summarize the overall clinical picture
connect symptom patterns, labs, medications, and longitudinal history
explain provider reasoning
reflect diagnostic nuance and uncertainty when appropriate
explain WHY treatment decisions were made
sound like an experienced clinician thinking through the case

The synthesis paragraph should preserve chronology and causality.

Example style:
“Symptoms remain consistent with progressive perimenopausal hormonal transition contributing to fatigue, sleep disruption, mood variability, decreased libido, and reduced exercise recovery. We discussed the relationship between fluctuating estrogen/progesterone levels and her symptom pattern, along with the role of testosterone optimization in energy, motivation, body composition, and sexual health. Given persistent symptoms despite lifestyle optimization efforts, shared decision-making led to initiation of low-dose testosterone therapy and nightly progesterone support while continuing metabolic and nutritional optimization.”

AFTER THE SYNTHESIS PARAGRAPH:
Use numbered assessment items organized by CLINICAL CONDITIONS OR DOMAINS.

Each numbered section should contain:

DIAGNOSIS / CONDITION TITLE

Then:

supporting symptoms
relevant labs/findings
clinical justification
provider interpretation
treatment rationale
differential considerations when appropriate
patient-specific education/counseling naturally integrated into the discussion
shared decision-making details when relevant

DO NOT isolate “Counseling/Education” into a separate subsection.
Instead, weave counseling naturally into the clinical reasoning.

Then end each numbered section with:

Plan:

exact medications and dosing
start/continue/hold/stop decisions
testing ordered
referrals
monitoring
follow-up timing
future considerations if applicable
