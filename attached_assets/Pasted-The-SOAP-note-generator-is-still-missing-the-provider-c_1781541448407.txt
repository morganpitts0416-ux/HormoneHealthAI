The SOAP note generator is still missing the provider clinical reasoning and shared decision-making layer.

Do not only document the final plan. The note must capture why the provider chose that plan, what options were discussed, what tradeoffs were explained, and what the patient decided.

Add a required “clinical reasoning preservation” rule:

When the transcript includes provider education, clinical explanation, analogies, treatment rationale, risks/benefits, or shared decision-making, preserve the clinically relevant meaning in the HPI and Assessment/Plan.

Examples:

* If estrogen is started to “cushion” estrogen drops, document that the provider suspects symptoms may correlate with fluctuating/dropping estrogen and is using low-dose transdermal estradiol to reduce symptom volatility, not simply to “optimize estrogen.”
* If the patient has had a hysterectomy but retained ovaries, document that she may still experience perimenopausal hormonal fluctuation despite no uterus.
* If testosterone options are discussed, document prior routes tried, why they may not have worked, and why the selected route was chosen.
* If GLP-1 dose reduction is discussed, document the suspected dose-related side effects and the reasoning for reducing to a better-tolerated maintenance dose.
* If mammogram, supplements, injections, or follow-up labs are discussed, include them in the plan when clinically relevant.

Add an anti-boilerplate rule:
Do not allow long default legal/compliance language to dominate the Assessment/Plan. If required, place it in a compact medication justification section or collapse it into 1–2 concise sentences unless the provider specifically requires full text.

The Assessment/Plan should not read like:
“Start medication. Follow up.”

It should read like:
“Provider suspects symptoms are related to estrogen fluctuation/drop pattern despite estradiol level not being frankly low. Discussed that low-dose transdermal estradiol may provide a stabilizing baseline/cushion to reduce hot flashes, joint pain, mood volatility, and energy crashes. Patient agrees to trial patch.”

Before finalizing, check:

1. Did we capture the provider’s thought process?
2. Did we capture the reason behind each medication change?
3. Did we capture options discussed but deferred?
4. Did we remove unnecessary boilerplate that makes the note less clinically useful?
5. Could another provider understand the plan without hearing the conversation?

Add a grounded clinical reasoning rule:

The SOAP generator must document provider reasoning when it is present in the transcript, but it must never fabricate detailed reasoning that was not stated or reasonably inferable.

There are two allowed reasoning types:

1. Explicit reasoning
   Use this when the provider clearly explains why they are recommending something.

Example:
Provider says estrogen patch may help “cushion” estrogen drops and reduce hot flashes/mood volatility.
Document: “Provider discussed that low-dose transdermal estradiol may help stabilize estrogen fluctuation/drop symptoms.”

2. Obvious clinical inference
   Use this only when the reason is medically direct and clearly supported by the encounter data.

Example:
Blood pressure is elevated and provider says, “Let’s start losartan.”
Document: “Start losartan for elevated blood pressure.”

Example:
A1C is elevated and provider says, “Let’s start metformin.”
Document: “Start metformin for elevated A1C/insulin resistance.”

Do not invent nuanced reasoning, differential diagnoses, risk-benefit analysis, or future concerns unless they were actually discussed.

Avoid phrases like:

* “Provider suspects...”
* “Likely due to...”
* “Discussed risks and benefits...”
* “This was chosen because...”
* “Concern for...”
  unless the transcript supports that statement.

When reasoning is not explicit but the medication/problem connection is obvious, use simple grounded language:
“Started X for Y.”
“Adjusted X due to reported side effect Y.”
“Ordered X to evaluate Y.”
“Referred to X for Y.”

When the provider’s reasoning is explicit, preserve it.
When the reasoning is obvious, document it simply.
When the reasoning is unclear, do not guess.

Final check:
For every Assessment/Plan statement, ask:

1. Was this explicitly stated?
2. Is this an obvious direct clinical inference?
3. Or am I making up reasoning?

If the answer is #3, remove it.
