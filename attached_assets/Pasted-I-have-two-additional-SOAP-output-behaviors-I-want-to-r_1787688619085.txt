I have two additional SOAP-output behaviors I want to refine, but analyze the existing shared Mode B SOAP prompt for conflicts before implementing anything.

1. Care Plan section function

The Care Plan is still too long and often repeats the detailed Plan.

I want the section distinction to be:

Plan: complete clinician-facing management details, including treatment decisions, relevant administration details, monitoring, safety information, and follow-up.
Care Plan: practical actions the patient needs to carry out after the visit.

The Care Plan should not become another abbreviated A&P or repeat rationale, Future Considerations, extensive counseling, or every detail already documented in the Plan.

It should retain clinically necessary patient actions such as:

medications to start/stop/change;
essential administration instructions;
testing or imaging the patient needs to complete;
monitoring/follow-up;
safety-critical precautions.

Closely related actions may be combined when doing so does not lose clinically meaningful or safety-critical information.

Do not solve this with a generic instruction such as “be concise” or an arbitrary item/word limit. This should be a section-function rule based on what belongs in Care Plan versus Plan.

2. Do not narrate missing prescription details

When a treatment decision is clearly established but the transcript does not verbalize every prescription-level detail, document the treatment using only the details that were established.

Do not add transcript-auditor or reconciliation language such as:

“dose to be verified”
“exact dose requires confirmation”
“exact dose not captured”
“exact ingredient concentrations require confirmation from the prescription record”
“verify dose before initiation”
similar language merely because a dose, concentration, quantity, or complete SIG was not spoken aloud.

The SOAP note does not need to reproduce every field that will appear on the prescription.

Example:

Transcript establishes that testosterone injections will be initiated twice weekly but does not establish the mg dose.

Prefer:
Initiate testosterone IM twice weekly.

Do not write:
Initiate testosterone IM twice weekly; exact dose requires confirmation from the prescription record.

And never infer or invent the missing dose.

Similarly, if a compounded estrogen/testosterone preparation is clearly being initiated but the exact concentrations are not verbalized, document the established treatment/route/frequency without adding a warning that the concentrations need to be confirmed.

Important safety distinction: This rule applies when the treatment decision itself is clear and only prescription-level details are absent from the transcript. It must NOT suppress a provider_review_flag or appropriate uncertainty documentation when there is a genuine clinically consequential ambiguity—for example:

conflicting doses are stated;
two routes remain genuinely possible;
it is unclear whether the medication was started versus merely discussed;
the final treatment decision cannot be resolved;
an ambiguity could materially affect patient safety.

In other words:

missing detail ≠ clinical ambiguity.

Document the known treatment without inventing the missing detail. Reserve review/verification language for genuine unresolved clinical decisions.

Before implementing, identify:

which existing prompt/QA rules are currently causing the verbose Care Plan;
which existing rules are causing “dose requires confirmation” / “exact concentration requires confirmation” language;
whether either proposed change conflicts with medication fidelity, Four-Location, Final Plan Precedence, or QA safeguards;
the smallest prompt-only changes needed to correct both behaviors without reducing clinical completeness or safety.

Do not implement or deploy yet.
