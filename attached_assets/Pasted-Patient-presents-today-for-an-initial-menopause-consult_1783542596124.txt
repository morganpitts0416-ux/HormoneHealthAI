Patient presents today for an initial menopause consultation seeking evaluation for fatigue, decreased energy, diminished libido, and persistent low sexual desire. Her primary goal for today's visit was to pursue estrogen and testosterone pellet therapy after hearing positive experiences from her sister, who receives pellet therapy in another state.

Patient reports a history of hot flashes that improved after previously starting estrogen therapy. She has also previously been prescribed oral estrogen/testosterone therapy and vaginal hormone therapy but states these treatments have provided little improvement in energy or libido. During today's visit, she acknowledged that she has been taking her hormone medications inconsistently, estimating that she often takes them only intermittently and sometimes goes a week or longer without taking them. She stated that one of the primary reasons she desired pellet therapy was because she struggles with medication adherence and felt pellets would eliminate the need to remember daily medication.

Past medical history was reviewed in detail and is significant for chronic kidney disease followed annually by nephrology, prior abdominal aortic aneurysm with iliac artery disease requiring surgical repair, hyperlipidemia, autoimmune disease with chronically elevated inflammatory markers, history of skin cancer treated with Mohs surgery, mild obstructive sleep apnea with intolerance to CPAP therapy, anxiety, depression, and prior hysterectomy with preservation of both ovaries.

Medication history was reviewed. Patient reports she has previously been advised to begin statin therapy but has declined treatment because of concerns regarding potential long-term adverse effects. She reports family history of Alzheimer's disease and expressed concern regarding long-term medication use.

Comprehensive laboratory results obtained prior to today's visit were reviewed in detail. Discussion included lipid profile, ApoB, Lipoprotein(a), inflammatory markers, thyroid function, vitamin levels, metabolic screening, kidney function, and reproductive hormone testing.

Laboratory findings demonstrate elevated LDL cholesterol, elevated non-HDL cholesterol, elevated ApoB, elevated hs-CRP, vitamin B12 deficiency, borderline folate status, vitamin D level of 41 ng/mL, and hemoglobin A1c of 5.5%. Her thyroid function was within normal limits. Hormonal evaluation demonstrates a postmenopausal pattern with FSH 58 mIU/mL, estradiol 8 pg/mL, progesterone 0.3 ng/mL, total testosterone 11.5 ng/dL, low free testosterone, and low-normal SHBG.

Using her laboratory values and cardiovascular risk profile, her ASCVD risk calculator demonstrated an estimated 10-year cardiovascular risk of approximately 11.7% with an estimated 30-year risk of approximately 30.8%. We discussed that this represents a significant cardiovascular risk profile that warrants aggressive risk factor modification.

We discussed recommendations to address modifiable cardiovascular risk factors including lipid-lowering therapy, correction of vitamin deficiencies, optimization of metabolic health, and appropriately monitored hormone replacement therapy. Patient again stated that she does not wish to take statin therapy.

Regarding hormone replacement, we discussed that she is an appropriate candidate for menopausal hormone therapy based upon both symptoms and laboratory findings. However, I explained that I was not comfortable initiating pellet hormone therapy during today's initial consultation due to her elevated cardiovascular risk profile, history of vascular disease, and the inability to titrate or discontinue hormone exposure once pellets have been inserted.

I explained that my recommendation would be to begin with alternative hormone replacement modalities that allow gradual titration and close monitoring while simultaneously addressing her cardiovascular risk factors. I further explained that pellet therapy was not being categorically ruled out in the future, but that additional risk-benefit discussion and careful consideration would be necessary before proceeding.

Patient repeatedly stated that pellet therapy was the only treatment she wished to pursue because she would not reliably take medications consistently. She stated that if pellet therapy was not offered there was no reason to continue treatment.

Patient became visibly emotional and requested to leave the visit before counseling could be completed. I made multiple attempts to continue the discussion, answer additional questions, review alternative treatment options, and explain my medical reasoning. I also explained that this decision was based on patient safety and my responsibility to fully discuss risks and benefits prior to initiating treatment. Patient declined further discussion, elected to terminate the visit, and left the office before a treatment plan could be established. She was advised that she is welcome to return for further discussion should she desire to continue evaluation and treatment.
